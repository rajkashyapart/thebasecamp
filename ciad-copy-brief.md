# ciad copy brief

Fill this in and I rewrite the page from it. Type under each arrow.

**Answer plainly. Don't polish.** Every time you've rewritten my copy you've
stripped the cleverness out and been right — so give me the flat version and
let me do the shaping. Fragments and half sentences are fine. Skip anything
you don't have an answer to rather than inventing one.

The old copy is in [ciad-copy-archive.md](ciad-copy-archive.md) if you want to
react to something specific instead of starting cold.

---

## A. the person reading it

**1. Name one real client who was a perfect fit. What did they do, and how big
were they when they came to you?**

→

**2. What did they say in the first call — in their words, not yours? The
complaint, the frustration, the thing they were embarrassed about.**

→

**3. What had they already tried before you, and what specifically went wrong
with it?**

→

**4. What do they actually want at the end of it? Not "growth" — the real
thing. More inbound? To stop thinking about it? To look serious to investors?**

→

**5. What's the objection you hear most often, in their exact words?**

→

**6. What do people get wrong about what you do? The assumption you have to
correct on every call.**

→

---

## B. things that can be pointed at

Harry Dry's second test: can I falsify it. Numbers and receipts beat adjectives.
Anything with a number in it is worth more than three sentences without one.

**7. Best result you can name publicly. Client, what changed, over how long.**

→

**8. Any numbers you're sitting on you haven't used — views, inbound leads,
close rate, followers gained, revenue attributed, repeat clients?**

→

**9. How many shoot days have you actually run? How many videos have come out
of them?**

→

**10. How long has a client stayed, on average? Longest one?**

→

**11. Anything about the day itself that's specific and concrete — how many
setups, how many hours, how many videos off one day, what you turn up with?**

→

---

## C. what nobody else can say

Harry Dry's third test: if a competitor swapped their name in, would it still
read? If yes, it's dead copy.

**12. What do you do on a shoot day that another agency in bangalore genuinely
would not do?**

→

**13. Dhiman. What's the one thing about how he works that's actually unusual?**

→

**14. What do you refuse to do, that others happily do for money?**

→

**15. Something true about you that has no business being on a sales page but
makes people trust you anyway.**

→

---

## D. the five screens

The deck is a day. Each screen has one job. Tell me what each one has to land —
or write the line yourself if you already know it.

**16. `08:00` the promise.** Currently *"an outlier isn't luck. it's the one
that got planned."* Keep it, or what should the first thing they read be?

→

**17. `09:00` the brief.** The job of this screen is to make them realise the
problem isn't effort. What's the sharpest way you'd say that out loud to a
founder?

→

**18. `13:00` the shoot.** What does the day actually feel like for the client?
Walk me through it as if you were telling a friend.

→

**19. `19:00` the work.** Right now this screen is five reels and one line.
Should the reels be different ones? Which five, and does each need a name
under it?

→

**20. `the month after` the price.** Anything wrong with the tier names,
prices, or the one-liners under them? Is "most pick this" still true?

→

**21. Is there a sixth thing a founder needs before booking that isn't on any
of the five screens?**

→
